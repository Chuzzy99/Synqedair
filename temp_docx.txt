SYNQED AIR
“Travel Synqed”
Strategy, Product &amp; Build Document
A sister company to SynqedAI
Co-founder: Emmanuel “Chuzzy” Onyia
August 2026
1. Executive Summary
Synqed Air is a flight-booking app built for the diaspora and price-conscious traveler — the fourth business in the SynqedAI empire architecture, and the second consumer-facing product after AI Native Studio&apos;s Haven and Mavin. Where SynqedAI is the agency that funds the empire, Synqed Air is a bet that travel booking in and out of Africa is still full of removable friction: hidden fees, poor support after disruption, no diaspora-specific guidance, and no single trustworthy price.
The motto — “Travel Synqed” — is the product philosophy in two words: search, price, book, support, and the whole journey around a flight, synchronized into one simple experience instead of scattered across five apps, a GDS-era airline site, and a WhatsApp agent.
The plan is not to become another OTA. It is to start as one (MVP on Duffel, December 2026 launch) and grow into the transparent-pricing challenger and diaspora-corridor specialist — with the underlying architecture built from day one to eventually become travel infrastructure other fintechs and apps can build on.
2. Vision &amp; Positioning
2.1 The real job customers are hiring for
Per the Product Strategy Playbook&apos;s core philosophy: customers don&apos;t buy a category, they hire something to get a job done. Nobody&apos;s real goal is “book a flight” — it&apos;s get to a destination, spend as little as possible, avoid stress, and trust that everything will go smoothly. Synqed Air&apos;s competition is therefore every substitute a traveler reaches for today, not just other flight apps.
Substitutes and where they fail
Airline websites — no price comparison, confusing fare rules
Wakanow and other OTAs — hidden charges, inconsistent support
Google Flights — no booking, support, or diaspora guidance
WhatsApp travel agents — fast but unverifiable, scam risk
Physical travel agencies — slow, no price transparency
Friends/family booking on your behalf — doesn&apos;t scale, no accountability
Common friction across all of them
Not knowing if you&apos;re getting the best price
Hidden charges revealed only at checkout
Poor support when a flight is delayed or cancelled
Having to check multiple sites to feel confident
Complicated refunds or ticket changes
Fear of scams, especially with informal agents
No guidance tailored to a diaspora traveler&apos;s specific needs (visas, corridors, currencies)
2.2 Positioning statement
“We remove every source of friction from travel — before, during, and after the flight.”
The external-facing story, already locked for the Dec 31 PR launch, is deliberately not a “first AI flight booking app” claim — that&apos;s already taken by Flightpadi&apos;s Ava, and a journalist could disprove it in one search. Instead:
Positioning: “The transparent-pricing challenger” and diaspora-corridor specialist.
Every piece of outreach and content should carry the substitute-analysis story — hidden fees, poor support, no diaspora focus among existing players — not a superiority claim about AI.
2.3 Advantage vs. unfair advantage
An advantage (price, UI, support hours) is something a competitor can copy within a product cycle. An unfair advantage — an ecosystem, a data loop, a trust relationship, a network effect — is what Synqed Air should be built toward. Two are already visible in the current plan:
Provider-agnostic architecture — the backend is built as Flight Service → Provider Interface → DuffelAdapter (with room for FutureAdapter) from day one, not hard-wired to Duffel. This is what makes it possible to eventually flip the model: instead of Synqed Air consuming Duffel/Amadeus/Travelport, other companies consume a Synqed Air API.
Diaspora-corridor focus — international-card Paystack fees (3.9% + ₦100, uncapped) are exactly the friction point generic OTAs ignore because the segment is narrower. Owning that corridor&apos;s pricing clarity and trust is harder to retrofit than to copy a UI.
A single feature claim (“cheaper flights”) is deliberately avoided — the advantage is framed broadly, across the whole journey, which is what keeps the roadmap (Section 8) coherent as the product grows past flights alone.
Potential competitive advantages (feature layer)
AI Travel Advisor — answer questions like “I have ₦500,000, what&apos;s the cheapest way to Nairobi next month?” Become a travel decision engine, not just a booking engine.
Total Price Transparency — show every fee upfront, no hidden charges, ever.
Instant Human Support — every customer reaches a real person within 2 minutes.
Smart Refund Assistant — AI helps users request refunds, change flights, and understand airline policies.
Africa-first Experience — local payment methods, regional airlines, visa guidance, airport information, local currencies.
Travel Companion — after booking, help with visa reminders, check-in reminders, gate changes, weather, baggage rules, airport navigation.
The real unfair advantage: the travel operating system
Rather than building another booking agency, the aim is the operating system for the whole journey:
“From the moment you decide to travel until you arrive at your destination, you never need another travel app.”
Flights are only the beginning — the ecosystem can eventually include hotels, airport transfers, visa information, insurance, eSIMs, currency conversion, AI itinerary planning, travel alerts, loyalty rewards, and corporate travel. Competitors can copy a single feature; it is much harder to copy an entire ecosystem that customers rely on.
North Star
Don&apos;t ask “Who are my competitors?” Ask “How else are customers solving this problem, where do those solutions fall short, and how can we become the simplest, most trusted solution?” Those gaps are where every competitive advantage comes from.
2.4 Applied product principles
Every screen and flow should be built against the Playbook&apos;s reduce/clarify/simplify standard:
Instead of 500 flight options → surface Best Value, Fastest, and Cheapest first
Instead of making users compare baggage policies → summarize them in plain English
Instead of making users search visa requirements → tell them automatically based on passport and destination
Instead of making users remember check-in times → remind them at the right moment
Instead of making users contact support after a cancellation → proactively present their options
3. Product &amp; Technical Architecture
3.1 Framework decision
One app, one framework. Dev 1 builds the actual mobile app in Flutter — a complete cross-platform toolkit (one codebase, iOS + Android), so nothing is missing by not using React Native. Dev 2&apos;s React Native background is really a JavaScript/TypeScript background, which maps directly onto the Node.js backend: AI advisor orchestration, flight aggregator integration, and payments. This was already Dev 2&apos;s role in the original plan — only the mobile framework changed, and nobody has to learn a new stack.
System diagram
Flutter app (Dev 1)  —HTTPS/JSON→  Node.js backend (Dev 2)
Backend integrates: Claude API (AI advisor parsing) · Duffel API (flight search) · Paystack API (payments) · Postgres (bookings, users).
The Flutter app never talks to Claude, Duffel, or Paystack directly — it only ever calls the Node backend. This keeps API keys off the device and keeps ranking logic, fee calculation, and pricing rules in one place instead of duplicated across clients.
What&apos;s already built
synqedair_flutter/ — Flutter starter for Dev 1: installs clean, three real screens (Home, Results, Companion) built to the approved design, wired for an API base URL, currently on mock data.
synqedair-backend/ — Node.js + TypeScript starter for Dev 2: installs clean, type-checks clean, boots and responds. Real Claude, Duffel, and Paystack clients are implemented; auth is stubbed.
Provider architecture — the one non-negotiable decision
Even though Duffel is the only provider for the MVP, the backend must not be architected as “everything is Duffel.” Instead:
Flight Service → Provider Interface → DuffelAdapter | FutureAdapter → Duffel
This keeps the December MVP simple while leaving the system structurally ready to add Amadeus, Travelport, or NDC sources later without a rewrite — see the roadmap in Section 8.
Item to verify before writing production logic against it
The Duffel client in the backend starter is written from documented API patterns but hasn&apos;t been run against a real Duffel account — nor has the Paystack client been run against a real transaction. Both type-check clean, but exact field names must be confirmed against current docs (duffel.com/docs, paystack.com/docs) in week 1, since APIs shift.
3.2 API contract (v0.1)
Full types live in src/types/index.ts (backend) and lib/models/*.dart (Flutter). Rule: whoever changes a field updates both in the same PR — a rename on one side and not the other is the #1 way two people building in parallel end up blocked on each other in week 6.

3.3 Local setup
Dev 2 (backend) runs first — Dev 1 depends on it:
cd synqedair-backend &amp;&amp; npm install &amp;&amp; cp .env.example .env  (fill in real keys)  &amp;&amp; npm run dev  → http://localhost:4000
Dev 1 (Flutter):
cd synqedair_flutter &amp;&amp; flutter pub get &amp;&amp; flutter run --dartdefine=API_BASE_URL=http://localhost:4000
If Dev 1 is on a physical device, localhost won&apos;t reach a backend running on Dev 2&apos;s machine — point API_BASE_URL at a shared dev server or a tunnel (ngrok or similar) once past day-one local testing.

5.3 Transaction fees (Paystack, verified current Nigeria rates)
Local cards: 1.5% + ₦100, capped at ₦2,000 per transaction — cheap relative to a flight fare
International cards: 3.9% + ₦100, uncapped — matters a lot for diaspora bookers. A ₦400,000 flight paid on a UK-issued card costs ~₦15,760 in fees alone
7.5% VAT applies on top of Paystack&apos;s fee itself (not on the transaction)
No setup fee, no monthly fee for Paystack
Pricing implication: absorb local-card fees into margin (capped and small), but factor international-card fees explicitly into diaspora-corridor pricing, since that&apos;s where the real cost sits.

7. Travel Distribution Primer: GDS, Aggregators, NDC &amp; Duffel
This section exists so every team member — not just the backend dev — shares the same mental model of who Synqed Air is actually connecting to and why.
7.1 The four building blocks
Airline — owns the actual flight, seats, prices, baggage rules, and booking system (e.g., Qatar Airways).
GDS (Global Distribution System) — large travel-distribution infrastructure/network that airlines connect to and travel sellers query instead of contacting every airline individually. Examples: Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport. These are competitors to each other.
Aggregator — a layer that combines content from multiple sources (GDS, NDC, direct airline connections, other suppliers) and presents it through one unified interface. GDS and aggregator are not opposites — an aggregator can consume GDS content without replacing a GDS.
NDC (New Distribution Capability) — not a human-language translator; a standardized format/rules for how an airline and a travel seller exchange richer flight offers and orders consistently.
7.2 Where Duffel fits
Duffel is not “a smaller Amadeus,” and it is not simply superior to a GDS — it is a different layer built for a different customer. Duffel connects directly to airline systems (often via NDC) and wraps that access in one modern, developer-friendly REST API. Conceptually:
Airlines → [NDC / Direct / GDS / other sources] → Duffel → One simple API → Synqed Air
Why Duffel for the MVP, specifically
A small software team needs flight inventory and booking capability through an API integrable into a Node.js backend — not a multi-month traditional GDS integration.
Duffel handles much of the underlying airline-distribution complexity so the team can focus on search, AI parsing, booking UX, and payments.
This is a fit-for-stage decision, not a claim that Duffel is technologically ahead of Amadeus/Sabre/Travelport — those remain deeper, more enterprise-oriented infrastructure with broader airline/hotel/car coverage.

1. Duffel — searching flights, offers, booking, ticketing, cancellations

3. GDS concepts — PNRs, fare rules, ticketing, queues, exchanges, refunds

8. Growth Roadmap: From Aggregator Consumer to Travel Infrastructure
The provider-agnostic backend architecture (Section 3.1) exists to make this progression possible without a rewrite at any stage.
Phase 1 — MVP: Duffel
Get Synqed Air working: search → price → book → pay → ticket → support. Duffel sandbox integration in October, Paystack booking/payment integration following it.
Phase 2 — After launch: Duffel + another supplier
Redundancy and potentially better inventory by adding a second source behind the same Provider Interface.
Phase 3 — Growth: Evaluate Amadeus or Travelport
Not because Duffel becomes bad — because real-world volume can now show whether another source gives better routes, fares, availability, ticketing options, commercial terms, or reliability.
Phase 4 — NDC
Connect to selected airline NDC content directly where it makes commercial sense.
Phase 5 — Direct airline relationships
At sufficient scale, airlines may want a direct commercial relationship — negotiated content, negotiated fares, better commissions, exclusive offers.
Phase 6 — Synqed Air API
The turnaround: instead of Synqed Air consuming Duffel/Amadeus/Travelport/NDC/airline infrastructure, Synqed Air becomes infrastructure itself — a Synqed Air API that fintechs, banks, and other travel apps build their own customer experiences on top of.
Summary progression: Duffel gets you into the market → multiple suppliers make you stronger → Amadeus/Travelport expand distribution options → NDC/direct airline connections deepen supply → direct airline relationships improve commercial position → the Synqed Air API eventually turns the company into the infrastructure.
